How it helps

You see the shape of a thought. A list hides how ideas connect. A branching map shows which directions you’ve opened, which you’ve skipped, and where one thought leads to another.

You get fresh angles without losing control. Claude suggests connections you might not have reached on your own. They stay dashed and separate from your thoughts until you decide to adopt them.

You can see how an idea changed. Snapshots keep dated copies of your map, so you can return to an earlier direction or notice what you dropped.

It suits early research questions, writing, naming, planning, or any problem you’re stuck on.

Value Compass

What is becoming more important to you? Compare your remembered priorities from five years ago with today, then compare both with the choices you actually make. Return in a few months to observe changes—not to receive a grade.

0 = not important or rarely expressed · 10 = central to my life. The past is a retrospective estimate, not a measurement taken five years ago.
